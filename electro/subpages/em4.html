EM4 - Plasma Globe

Demonstrates fundamental principles of high-voltage electricity, ionization, and thermodynamics.

The sealed glass sphere is evacuated of regular air and refilled with a low-pressure mixture of noble gases (most commonly neon, argon, xenon, and krypton). Because the gas pressure inside is lower than the atmospheric pressure outside, it takes less voltage to rip electrons away from the gas atoms. Plasma Formation: The massive voltage radiating from the center electrode strips electrons away from these noble gas atoms, transforming the gas into plasma—a glowing mixture of free electrons and positively charged ions. As the free electrons zoom toward the outer glass sphere, they bump into other noble gas atoms. These collisions temporarily energize ("excite") the electrons remaining inside the atoms. When those electrons drop back down to their normal energy levels, they release energy in the form of light (photons). The specific colors you see—such as pink, purple, or blue—depend entirely on the specific mixture of noble gases inside the globe.
