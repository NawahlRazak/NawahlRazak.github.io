EM129 - Magnet down a tube

Shows self-inductance. A strong neodymium magnet falls through a copper tube in slow motion due to induction.

Drop two similar-looking disks down a magnetic tube. One is conducting, so the force due the magnetic field slows down its decent. The other does not conduct electricity, so it is unaffected by the magnetic field and falls normally. The magnet never actually touches the inside walls of the copper tube, and copper itself is non-magnetic, meaning there is zero physical attraction or mechanical friction slowing it down. eddy currents. Lenz's law, the magnetic field produced by these induced eddy currents always opposes the change that created it. The current creates a temporary magnetic field that pushes back against the falling magnet, slowing its descent.
